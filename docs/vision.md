# Vision

## The promise

Most cozy games ask the player to restore or save a town. **Rooted** asks how a person becomes part of one.

Rowan begins as a guest. Art offers work at her bar and a room upstairs, with no demand that Rowan explain themself or promise to stay. Belonging appears through accumulated changes rather than a declared milestone: someone stops knocking, a neighbor walks behind the bar to borrow sugar, children ask Rowan for help, and other people begin calling the upstairs room Rowan's room.

No achievement needs to announce that transformation. The player notices.

## Emotional center

Before the game begins, Rowan is on the brink of suicide and a nervous breakdown. Art, a high-ranking reaper assigned to wait for Rowan's death, intervenes. She claims a vague connection to Rowan's family and offers a job, housing, and a way to leave without first explaining how desperate things have become.

Art does not save Rowan because they are useful. She does not recruit them for a magical crisis, expect gratitude, or demand that they make the rescue worthwhile. She interrupts one ending and gives Rowan a practical next step. Living afterward remains difficult, ordinary work.

This foundation requires restraint. Rowan's crisis is not a collectible secret, friendship reward, or dramatic twist. Art's intervention does not cure Rowan, and being alive creates no debt to her.

## Seasons, not an ending

Character and community arcs may have climaxes, but the game continues into another spring. The closest equivalent to resolution is that Rowan's departure stops being the community's default assumption. Even that emerges through play rather than a final ceremony.

## Art's bar

The bar is the town's living room: workplace, meeting hall, shelter, rumor exchange, and a place where groups repeatedly cross paths. At first it is unmistakably Art's bar. Over years, Rowan's habits appear behind the counter and in other people's expectations, without a required promotion or ownership scene.

## Storytelling approach

The game establishes dependable routines before unusual details catch against them. Art's secret belongs in dramatic irony and cumulative evidence: a detail she should not know, shifting accounts of Rowan's relatives, a ticket purchased too early, an oblique remark from another reaper, or a record bearing Rowan's arrival date.

Each clue should support a mundane reading. Art never winks at the audience or gives a confirming monologue. Rowan may never name the truth with certainty, and the game never needs to reward the player for solving it.

## Character and dialogue principles

- Characters have lives and obligations beyond Rowan.
- Intimacy does not automatically become romance.
- Magical beings are not automatic authorities on morality or faith.
- People misread, deflect, interrupt, and understand imperfectly.
- Emotional meaning lives in concrete behavior and changing routine rather than explanation.
- Humor grows from character and practical underreaction to fantasy oddities.

## Development principles

Build outward from story needs. Systems should preserve distinctions among what happened, what Rowan witnessed, what each character believes, what the town records, and what the player infers.

No game engine has been selected. Engine choice follows a clearer narrative scope, interaction model, content pipeline, and prototype requirement.

Rooted should not become a chosen-one story, a debt narrative, a world-saving plot, a mystery box built to confirm Art's identity, or a life sim whose definitive ending makes continued seasons feel non-canonical.
